### CPU rewrite (test/cpurw 33e70d4ad, in all24): a title patch, in memory, opt-in
The title was built for the console's Zen 2. On this PC's Intel CPU every `insertq` (SSE4a), `clzero`, `monitorx` and
`mwaitx` it runs is an invalid-opcode exception that the loader emulates (`crates/victory-loader/src/crash.rs`):
all23-s-reaper1 counts, a second, 22,000-23,500 for clzero, 15,000-20,000 each for monitorx and mwaitx, about 500 for
insertq. With the switch the 41 known sites (`crates/victory-game/src/cpu_sites.rs`: 19 insertq, 10 clzero, 8 clwb,
2 monitorx, 2 mwaitx) are rewritten once, in the mapped image, where the other title patches are made: after the
image is mapped and before its imports are bound, its DLLs loaded or its main thread made, so no thread is in a site.
No file changes. Per site: only if this CPU lacks the instruction (CPUID; this PC has `clwb`, so its 8 sites are left
alone), only if the bytes there are the table's, decode as the row's instruction, what would move with it is
register-only and no branch lands inside. A site that fails a check is left to the exception path and named in one
line. `VICTORY_TITLE_PATCHES=0` turns it off with the others. One line at load (stdout and the log) says how many
sites were rewritten per kind, how many the CPU runs itself and how many are left and why; the per-second "AMD-only
instructions emulated" counts stay, and fall to nothing for the kinds rewritten.
Under the write watch: a clzero stub zeroes its line with ordinary SSE2 stores, so into a block the guards made
read-only it faults as any store of the title does (an access violation; `watch.rs`'s handler goes by the address
written, not by the code), the block is opened and the store runs again. Tested with the real guard (`watch.rs`,
`a_rewritten_clzero_writes_a_guarded_block_like_any_store`) and in the loader with every register compared (a read-only
page, a `PAGE_GUARD` page, write-watched memory).
DEFAULT ON in this branch, a fix of the exception path itself, with or without the rewrite: `VICTORY_CLZERO_PROTECTED`.

Notes for stream3:
- `VICTORY_SECTION_MB=2` and `VICTORY_XMEM_PERSISTENT_VIEW=1` attack the same address-space and section locks from two
  sides: SECTION_MB makes each section 2 MiB (so the title's 6.1 GiB is ~3,100 sections and the large-page worker
  looks through a few dozen views of one), the kept view takes our per-request views away. Together: one kept view of
  2 MiB per section touched by DirectStorage (~3,100 views at most, the sections' total size in address space, no
  extra commit). Without SECTION_MB the kept view of the largest section is 6.1 GiB of address space at once.

  section's kept view (with SECTION_MB=2 more requests cross: they still map nothing).
